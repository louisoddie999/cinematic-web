Step 0 · Before You Generate Anything

Soul ID Setup

Soul ID locks your face into every generated shot. Without it, Higgsfield uses a generic character. With it, you are the armored warrior in every frame.

⚡

Do this first — before anything else. Soul ID is the foundation of the entire shoot. All 8 shots reference your locked character. If Soul ID isn't set up, every shot drifts to a random face.

01

Go to higgsfield.ai → Soul ID

Find Soul ID in the left sidebar or top navigation. This is where you create a persistent character identity the model will use across all shots.

02

Upload 20–30 photos of yourself

Photo requirements:
· Mix of angles: front-facing, 3/4 left, 3/4 right, side profile
· Mix of lighting: daylight, indoor, bright, dimmer conditions
· Some with glasses or accessories if you wear them, some without
· Neutral expressions AND a few natural ones
· No heavy filters, no Facetune, no sunglasses — the model needs your actual face
· Photos from the last 2 years work best

03

Wait for training to complete

Training takes 5–15 minutes. You'll get a notification. Once complete, your Soul ID appears as a selectable character in the VIDEO tab and Cinema Studio.

04

Test with a single shot first

Before running the full 8-shot sequence, generate one test shot — a simple medium close-up of your character in normal clothes. If the face looks like you, Soul ID is working. If it drifts, upload 5–10 more photos focusing on your face at different angles.

Step 0b · Before Soul ID Test Shot

Generate Your Armor Reference Image

Create the armor design first. Then load it as @ref2 in CS3. This gives Cinema Studio a locked visual target for the armor design — without forcing a rigid end frame. The transformation feels organic, the armor looks exactly like yours.

🛡

Why reference instead of end frame? Using the armor as @ref2 gives CS3 the design language without locking every pixel of the final frame. The transformation sequence stays fluid and cinematic. CS3 interprets the design intent rather than snapping to a frozen image.

Where to generate

Higgsfield Image Generator

Or use Midjourney, Flux Dev, or Stable Diffusion. Any photorealistic model works — you just need the output image file.

How to use in CS3

Ref Slot 2 → @ref2

Upload generated armor image to Reference Slot 2 in CS3. Tag it as @ref2 in your video prompt wherever armor is described.

Generate the armor image

Paste the prompt above into your image generator of choice. Generate 4–8 variations. Pick the one that feels most like your armor — the design that resonates. You can iterate on the prompt: change color scheme, tweak the helmet style, adjust shoulder guard geometry.

02

Upload to CS3 as Reference Slot 2

In Cinema Studio 3.0, open the Reference panel. Upload your armor image to Slot 2. CS3 will tag it automatically — confirm it's labeled @ref2 in the reference panel.

03

Tag @ref2 in your video prompt

In the Main Video Prompt (see Prompts section below), wherever armor is described — add @ref2 inline. Example: "Armor plates materialize — gunmetal grey with midnight blue accents @ref2 — snapping onto the body with magnetic force." CS3's Cinematic Reasoning Engine reads the tag and pulls visual data from your armor image.

Step 1 · What You Feed Cinema Studio 3.0

Multimodal Input Stack

Cinema Studio 3.0 accepts up to 12 inputs simultaneously — 9 images, 3 video clips, 3 audio clips. Here is exactly what to load for this transformation trailer.

🖼

Reference Images

Up to 9 slots
  • Slot 1: Your Soul ID character sheet (auto-linked if Soul ID is active)
  • Slot 2: Armor reference — gunmetal/midnight blue tactical suit. Pull from concept art, game screenshots (Halo, Destiny, Iron Man)
  • Slot 3: Location/environment — dark rain-slicked rooftop, city glow below, storm approaching
  • Slot 4 (optional): Mood/lighting reference — a Marvel BTS still or a cinematography reference for the color grade
  • Use @ref1 @ref2 @ref3 tags in your text prompt to call each one
🎬

Video Reference Clips

Up to 3 slots
  • Clip 1 (optional but powerful): A Marvel trailer sequence — Iron Man suiting up, Black Panther transformation, Thor lightning reveal. CS3 reads the camera movement and energy rhythm and applies it to your shot
  • Clip 2 (optional): A slow-motion VFX reference — suit assembly, particle effects, energy surge
  • Even a 5-second clip is enough. CS3 extracts the motion language, not the content
🎵

Audio Input

Up to 3 slots
  • Option A (recommended): Upload a piece of cinematic music — Hans Zimmer style, 15–30 seconds. CS3 uses the beat to time the transformation peak and the hero reveal
  • Option B: No audio input — CS3 generates its own soundtrack in the Action genre
  • Option C: Use CS3's Native Audio — select a sound design style from the Audio tab. Choose "Epic Orchestra" or "Hybrid Trailer"
  • Music-first workflow: generating audio first and then letting the visuals sync to the beat produces the most cinematic result

Step 2 · Cinema Studio 3.0 Configuration

Panel Settings

Every dial set precisely for a Marvel-style hyper-real transformation. Copy these settings exactly before generating.

🎯

Path: higgsfield.ai → Cinema Studio 3.0 → New Project → set all values below before pressing Generate.

Model

Seedance 2.0

Most physics-aware, highest motion stability. Do not use Seedance 1.0.

Genre

Action

Action genre activates: high-contrast lighting, fast shutter-style motion blur, punchy color grade, cinematic character framing.

Duration

12 seconds

Maximum controllable range for a single transformation arc. CS3 handles up to 15s.

Output Resolution

4K Native

Seedance 2.0 outputs native 4K. Do not upscale — you lose the grain and lens characteristics.

Speed Ramp — Primary

Impact

Builds tension slowly, hits a peak moment with compressed speed, then eases into aftermath. Used for the transformation beat at 00:02.5.

Speed Ramp — Secondary

Bullet Time

Ultra slow-motion at the hero reveal (00:07–00:09). Character in full armor, camera orbiting. This is the money shot.

Camera Sensor

ARRI Alexa 35

Organic color science, natural grain structure, the sensor Marvel and DC actually use. Best cinematic response to dark scenes.

Lens

35mm Anamorphic

Wide enough for full-body shots, tight enough for the face reveal. Anamorphic gives horizontal lens flares on the armor's metallic edges.

Aperture

f/2.0 – f/2.8

Shallow depth of field. Background city lights become golden bokeh orbs. Character stays in crisp focus.

Color Grade

Teal + Orange

Classic blockbuster grade — skin tones pushed warm orange, backgrounds and shadows pushed cool teal. Request explicitly in your prompt.

Physics Engine

ON — High

Essential for armor assembly. Physics engine calculates momentum of plates snapping to body, cape/cloth dynamics, particle impacts.

Character Consistency

Soul ID — Active

Link your Soul ID character to every shot in the multi-shot panel. Your face persists across all 8 shots.

All 8 Speed Ramp Presets — Reference

Preset Behaviour Best Use in This Trailer
Linear Constant speed. No ramp. Avoid — no cinematic tension
Auto AI decides based on content Fallback when unsure
Flash In Starts fast, decelerates Opening shot — storm approach entering frame
Flash Out Starts slow, accelerates out Transition from calm to transformation beat
Slow-mo Sustained slow motion throughout Armor surface detail shots
Bullet Time Extreme slow-motion, camera orbits Hero reveal (00:07–00:09) — the money shot
Impact ★ Build → hit → slow aftermath Transformation peak (00:02.5–00:06) — primary preset
Ramp Up Slow start, accelerates to end Power demonstration walk (00:09–00:11)
